## Findings
1. Leaving aside evaluations on in-window mouse data, scFM is the only 2025-2026 population forecaster I found that reports a copy-last ("Naive") baseline at 2,000 or more genes. In log-normalised 2,000-HVG gene space, its Sinkhorn W2 on extrapolated time points is 27-36% below copy-last: zebrafish t=10/11, 600/653 against 888/944; Drosophila t=9/10, 530/683 against 825/969; Schiebinger reprogramming t=16-18, 112-121 against 161-170. scNODE sits within 1-10% of scFM, so any drift model fitted on 6-13 training snapshots [corrected in audit: was "9-16"; scFM trains one model per dataset on the complement of a single multi-holdout split, which leaves 7 zebrafish, 6 Drosophila and 13 reprogramming snapshots; the paper does not list them] wins this gain over the copy. The horizons are 1-4 steps past the last training snapshot [corrected in audit: was "1-3 steps"; reprogramming t=15 is held out too, so t=16-18 lie 2-4 steps, 24-48 h, past t=14] (1 h per Drosophila step and 12 h per reprogramming step, as scNODE describes the data) [2][21].
2. scTimeBench (ISMB 2026: ten methods, eight datasets, four species, next-time-point horizon, 2,000 HVGs except a whole-transcriptome pancreas set) has no copy-last or nearest-stage baseline. scIMF ranks first for forecasting (best Wasserstein and Hausdorff), with MIOFlow, scNODE and PI-SDE behind. Squidiff ranks first on Gaussian-kernel MMD (mean rank 1.0) but last on the other distances. OT methods lead on lineage, where most methods "often underperform" a correlation baseline [1].
3. CellTempo (18,791 protein-coding genes, NB decoder, more than 48M pseudotime trajectories built from more than 90M scBaseCount cells) beats a delta-extrapolation baseline only modestly, and the spreads overlap: pseudotime PCC 0.74±0.35 against 0.63±0.33, root-distance correlation 0.84±0.16 against 0.77±0.13. Its horizons are pseudotime steps, not days, and it reports no copy baseline [3].
4. For composition at a held-out stage (98 zebrafish cell types, 18-72 hpf, one stage held out per fold, about 70 training rows), zero-shot TabPFN reaches Spearman 0.808 and R² 0.704, and plain CatBoost 0.763 and 0.662. A Mean baseline reaches R² 0.352 overall and falls to -0.49 at 18 hpf, the backward-extrapolated stage. The trend model Prophet trails at Spearman 0.533, and the paper tests no copy-last baseline [4].
5. In per-type pseudobulk forecasting for unseen cell types (human trauma immune cells, four time points), genes whose temporal change agrees across types (DCI > 0.8) can be forecast: an RNN with Gaussian NLL reaches MASE 0.78, about 22% lower MAE than carry-forward, and MLP and transformer reach about 0.85. Below DCI 0.2, no model, linear included, beats carry-forward (MASE > 1) [5].
6. A temporal signature learned in one study transfers poorly: median error is 1.52 weeks within the study and 8.03 weeks on other studies. One integration-free elastic net on per-type pseudobulk pooled over 11 human studies [corrected in audit: was "13"; its 905 pseudobulks come from 11 training datasets, and two of the 13 were held out for validation] holds 2.65 weeks leave-study-out and 2.86 weeks on held-out cell types. Type-specific models are as accurate on other types as on their own (3.92 against 3.94 weeks, P = 0.25) [6].
7. The 2025-2026 unbalanced-transport methods set growth only from data on both sides of the gap. WFR-FM regresses growth onto d/dt ln m, which it derives from a static WFR semi-coupling between adjacent snapshots. It validates only interpolation, in 10-50 dimensions: held-out W1 0.298 against VGFM's 0.301 on EMT, and 10.157 against DeepRUOT's better 10.075 on embryoid bodies. MUST-FM takes endpoint masses from lineage-masked semi-couplings, and its only held-out test is on an in-window atlas [7][8].
8. The other routes to growth use cell numbers or gene signatures and are also interpolation-only. ARTEMIS fits kill rates to the change in sampled cell numbers between snapshots: held-out W is 9.05 against scNODE's 9.22 at one pancreatic day and 9.84 against 9.73 at the other. scMTG weights cells by z-scored proliferation and death gene sets: [removed in audit: W — a pooled benchmark count whose interpolation time points include mouse embryonic datasets inside our windows], and on its serum reprogramming data median LISI +13.0% and SCC +4.3% over the runner-up [corrected in audit: these two figures are on the Serum dataset only]. Neither has a naive baseline or tests growth extrapolated beyond the last snapshot [9][10].
9. No 2025-2026 temporal forecaster that I found measures sparsity or gene-gene covariance at a held-out time outside our windows. Generators that produce counts directly report only unconditional gains. On dentate gyrus, count-FM reaches W2 20.456 against scLDM's 20.739 and CFGen's 22.226, and MMD² 0.0185 against 0.0202 and 0.0361. On Tabula Muris, scLDM reaches W2 7.267 against CFGen's 11.658, and scDiffusion underestimates per-gene variance [18][19].
10. Developmental-time inference now transfers across datasets. Gene-Chronos (frozen, human-only Geneformer backbone) reaches Pearson 0.898 and RMSE 1.52 days across human embryo datasets, against TemporalVAE's 0.878 and 1.86. In zebrafish, leave-one-timepoint-out, it reaches Spearman 0.860 against 0.800 [12].
11. Sunbear transfers temporal profiles across sex, batch and modality with a cVAE: a sinusoidal time code, one-hot batch and condition, a time-adversarial identity embedding and a ZINB likelihood. Its baselines are the same type's pseudobulk at the previous or next time and the mean of the two. The code is Apache-2.0 and no weights are released. Its training atlases include in-window stages, so only a retrain on legal stages could be used [11].
12. PACE (NeurIPS 2026) reports a 23.7% average reduction in MMD, W1 and W2 against the strongest competitor over nine held-out reconstructions (embryoid bodies, iPSC, reprogramming) [corrected in audit: the nine reconstructions span seven datasets, which also include two non-biological controls (an ocean-particle benchmark and a synthetic bifurcation) and Open Problems multimodal data; the paper does not say which experiments the average covers]. On Drosophila extrapolation, LGP-OT's per-cell latent time and cell-type kernel gain 1-4% W2 over the better of PI-SDE and VGFM (31.88 against 32.80 at t=8). Neither paper compares against a naive baseline [15][16].
13. The pretrained temporal models are either possibly contaminated for our windows or built for other tasks. Chreode pretrains on a 2.4M-cell mouse embryonic atlas built from 7 datasets. CellTempo pretrains on scBaseCount (16,057 public datasets, species and stage mix not stated). MaxToki is human-only (Genecorpus-175M plus 22M aging cells). TabPFN-2.6 is trained purely on synthetic tasks and carries a non-commercial licence [13][3][14][23].
14. The field's 2026 review still names extrapolation as an open problem ("Evaluation metrics must extend beyond interpolation"). The newest growth and branching methods report interpolation only (USB's discrete birth-death jumps, GeoBridge) or no held-out time at all (DiffusionOT) [22][24][25][20].

## Deep Read Notes
### Source [1]: scTimeBench (ISMB 2026)
Key data: 10 methods (8 forecasting, 2 OT), 8 datasets, 4 species. Tasks are interpolation, extrapolation and joint, each at t -> t+1. Metrics are Wasserstein, Gaussian MMD, energy MMD and Hausdorff, reported only as mean ranks. scIMF is best overall; Squidiff is best on Gaussian MMD and worst elsewhere. There is no copy or nearest-stage baseline.
Key insight: the benchmark answers "which deep method", not "better than copying". Kernel-MMD and Wasserstein rankings disagree (Squidiff), so a model can win an RBF-MMD metric while losing on support.
Useful for: setting deep forecasters aside as a T1 lever, and as a caution that a published MMD win does not imply an mmd_u gain.

### Source [2]: scFM (copy-last-relative extrapolation)
Key data: 2,000 HVGs, library-size normalised then log1p. W2 (Sinkhorn) and mean pairwise l2 are computed in gene space after decoding from a 50-d latent. Held out (interpolation / extrapolation): ZB 4,6,8 / 10,11; DR 4,6,8 / 8,9,10; SC 5,10,15 / 16,17,18. Naive copies the last observed point. Copy-last also loses on interpolation (ZB t=4: 776 against 427).
Key insight: every drift model beats the copy when 6-13 snapshots [corrected in audit: was "9-16"; one model per dataset on the complement of the held-out set] trace a smooth drift; scFM's margin over scNODE is 1-10%. There is no ablation, code, sparsity, composition or emergence analysis, and the DR table lists t=8 twice.
Useful for: a copy-relative evaluation template. It also shows that the gain needs a dense ladder [audit: an inference; [2] has no ablation on ladder density], which we lack: E8.5, E9.5 and E10.5, with sampling changes between them.

### Source [4]: Tabular foundation models (composition at a held-out stage)
Key data: zebrafish zscape (about 2.7M cells, 28 perturbations, 18-72 hpf, 98 types), one stage withheld per fold. Spearman / R²: TabPFN 0.808/0.704, CatBoost 0.763/0.662, TabICL 0.741/0.604, Prophet 0.533/0.651; Mean R² 0.352 (-0.49 at 18 hpf). About 70 training rows per fold.
Key insight: a learned composition trend beats the mean only when many perturbation x stage rows exist [audit: an inference; [4] tests only its 70-80-row regime], and a dedicated time-series model (Prophet) does worse than general-purpose tabular learners.
Useful for: the T1 composition prior (legal route). The learner is free of window data (synthetic weights), but about 5 stage rows from two sampling regimes cannot identify a trend.

### Source [5]: Dynamic Consistency Index
Key data: human trauma immune scRNA-seq at Ctrl, <4 h, 24 h and 72 h. DCI is the mean pairwise cosine between per-type temporal-difference vectors. At DCI > 0.8 an RNN reaches MASE 0.78, MLP and transformer about 0.85; at DCI < 0.2 every model has MASE > 1. DCI from training types against all types: Spearman 0.923.
Key insight: carry-forward cannot be beaten for genes whose change is specific to one type, and agreement across types predicts which genes transfer.
Useful for: the gating logic of levers 1 and 3. It is one small human dataset, so the evidence is weak.

### Source [6]: Cell type-agnostic maturation clock (PLOS Biology)
Key data: 13 human fetal brain datasets, 2.89M cells, 905 per-type pseudobulks [audit: the 905 come from the 11 training datasets; two datasets were held out for validation]; elastic net keeps 462 of 10,957 genes. Leave-study-out error is 2.65 weeks; single-study models reach 1.52 weeks within their study and 8.03 weeks across studies. Type-specific models: 3.94 weeks on their own type, 3.92 on others (P = 0.25). Organoids R 0.91 and 0.89; the rank-normalised variant reaches R 0.52 on HNOCA.
Key insight: a temporal signature from one study carries a large study-specific error, pooling studies removes most of it, and neural types share a maturation axis.
Useful for: why a single external anchor (Feng) can mislead and why a second, independent legal source might help (lever 1), and the shared component (lever 3).

### Source [7]: WFR-FM (ICLR 2026)
Key data: the growth target is d/dt ln m_t, from a static WFR semi-coupling between adjacent snapshots (m0 = 1, m1 = gamma1/gamma0). One intermediate time held out, W1: EMT 0.298, EB 10.157 (DeepRUOT 10.075), CITE 37.221, mouse hematopoiesis 6.586. 10-50 dimensions; no naive baseline.
Key insight: growth comes from the masses on both sides of the gap, so it is undefined beyond the last snapshot. The method cannot set E10.5 -> E12.5 growth without in-window data.
Useful for: confirming the prunes of own-growth quotas and births.

### Source [9]: ARTEMIS (ISMB 2025)
Key data: a VAE with an unbalanced diffusion Schrödinger bridge. A kill-rate network is fitted to observed cell-count changes, with a heuristic prior equal to the share of features more than 2 SD from the next time's mean. 2,000 HVGs. On held-out intermediate times W against scNODE is mixed: 9.05 against 9.22, 9.84 against 9.73, and 28.96-31.3 against 29.2-31.8 on zebrafish.
Key insight: "growth" here is the number of cells sampled per snapshot. In VEC that number is set by the dataset's design, not by biology.
Useful for: a counter-example to inferring growth from cell numbers across our stages.

## SOTA-to-VEC mapping
**1. Latent drift forecasters: scFM [2]; scIMF, MIOFlow, scNODE, PI-SDE and Squidiff via [1]; PACE [15]; LGP-OT [16]; CellPace [17].**
- What it does: fits a velocity or drift field in a VAE or PCA latent space from many snapshots, rolls it forward, and decodes to log-normalised HVG expression.
- Evidence: scFM is 27-36% below copy-last on W2 at 1-4 steps (up to 24-48 h on reprogramming), with 6-13 training snapshots [corrected in audit: was "1-3 steps" and "9-16"], 2,000 HVGs, and no check of sparsity or composition [2]. scTimeBench and PACE have no naive baseline [1][15]. LGP-OT gains 1-4% over deep baselines [16]. CellPace was evaluated only on in-window mouse data, which is not recorded here [17].
- Board and group: T1 direction and DE recovery through the drift; mmd_u through shifted positions; covariance, which decoded dense values hurt.
- Legality: training on the released E8.5, E9.5 and E10.5 is legal. A15 does not apply. Composition stays untouched unless growth is added, and that lever is closed. GPU non-determinism conflicts with the freeze-morning hash check (TRIES). s2 is frozen.
- Licence: scFM has no code; scNODE is MIT (digest of 2026-10-19); PACE code is public (licence not checked); scIMF, LGP-OT and CellPace state none.
- Counted backtest: E10.5 from E9.5 and from E8.5. With only two transitions of our own, the learned drift is momentum, and the team's test-like readout ranks momentum below Feng (Spearman 0.40 against 0.80, PLAN).
- Expected gain: -3 to 0 T1 points. Submitting decoded dense cells costs variogram as pseudobulk_shift did (48.58 against the copy's 50, variogram skill 38.9, about -2.2 points). Using only the model's pseudobulk direction reproduces momentum, which Feng already beats.
- Repeats a prune: yes ("whole-ladder flow with growth", "decoded sampling", "cell-level Wasserstein momentum"). New: copy-relative numbers from dense ladders only; none of the pruning reasons is answered.

**2. Pretrained temporal world models: CellTempo [3], Chreode [13], MaxToki [14].**
- What it does: one-step or autoregressive generators of cell states, pretrained on large corpora.
- Evidence: CellTempo against a delta baseline, pseudotime PCC 0.74 against 0.63 with overlapping SDs [3]. Chreode reports no copy baseline [13]. MaxToki covers human aging (held-out ages r = 0.77) [14].
- Board and group: in principle T1 direction and DE recovery.
- Legality: nothing shows CellTempo (scBaseCount) or Chreode (a mouse embryonic atlas) to be free of E9.5-E13.5 mouse embryo data, so they are barred "via a pretrained model" unless provenance is proven (unclear at best). MaxToki is human-only, so it is allowed on the same footing as scGPT, but it does not address our question.
- Licence: none stated for any of the three.
- Counted backtest: none that is legal.
- Expected gain: 0 (unusable).
- Repeats a prune: the foundation-model screen of 2026-10-19 (Navigo and Chreode weights plausibly contaminated). New: CellTempo and MaxToki join the list; nothing changes.

**3. Unbalanced growth and births: WFR-FM [7], MUST-FM [8], ARTEMIS [9], scMTG [10], DiffusionOT [20], USB [24] (VGFM, DeepRUOT and TIGON already covered).**
- What it does: learns a velocity plus a growth field g(x,t) or kill rates. MUST-FM masks transitions by lineage; scMTG and WOT weight cells by proliferation and death signatures.
- Evidence: validation is interpolation-only. Growth targets come from the masses on both sides of the gap [7][8], from sampled cell numbers [9] or from gene-set z-scores [10]. DiffusionOT has no held-out test [20]. Nothing validates growth extrapolated past the last snapshot.
- Board and group: T1 composition, hence mmd_u (.30) and DE recovery (.25).
- Legality: composition moves only by the legal route (quotas at the frozen count from a prior the captain writes). The change in counts and shares from E9.5 to E10.5 reflects sampling (embryo portion against dissected heart), not growth. A signature-based growth rate from E10.5 uses legal data but gives a rate, not an identified change in share.
- Licence: code is public for WFR-FM, MUST-FM, ARTEMIS and scMTG, with no licence stated; the MUST-FM paper is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
- Counted backtest: no clean one; the E10.5-from-E9.5 truth carries the 64% against 19% cardiomyocyte confound.
- Expected gain: unknown sign; prior 0.
- Repeats a prune: yes ("own-growth quotas", whose growth is not identified; "births by the legal route"; "whole-ladder flow with growth"). New: the 2025-2026 literature confirms that no method identifies growth beyond the last snapshot, which strengthens these prunes.

**4. Generators that produce counts directly: count-FM [18], scLDM [19] (CFGen already covered).**
- What it does: generates integer counts, by birth-death jumps in count space (count-FM) or through NB decoders (scLDM).
- Evidence: gains in unconditional W2 and MMD only. Transport from P12 to P35 is judged qualitatively, and no paper measures count realism at a held-out time [18][19].
- Board and group: T1 covariance (variogram) and mmd_u.
- Legality: training on legal stages is fine. scLDM's weights come from the human CELLxGENE Census, so they are allowed but built for other tasks.
- Licence: no count-FM code found; scLDM code at czi-ai (licence not checked).
- Counted backtest: E10.5 from E9.5 could judge a count-space transport, but its drift would again be momentum.
- Expected gain: -1 to 0. Real-cell draws already carry E10.5's sparsity and covariance, NB resampling scored about 0 in the 2026-11-05 research, and a generator can at best reproduce E10.5.
- Repeats a prune: yes ("negative-binomial resampling", "latent Gaussian-OT decoding", "the same exact top-up on T1"). New: transport in count space keeps integer zeros, but there is still no held-out evidence.

**5. Tabular foundation models for composition: Palla et al. [4], TabPFN-2.6 [23].**
- What it does: zero-shot regression of composition vectors at a held-out stage.
- Evidence: on zebrafish 98-type composition, R² 0.70 against the Mean baseline's 0.35. CatBoost is about as good as TabPFN, and Prophet is worse [4].
- Board and group: T1 composition, hence mmd_u and DE recovery.
- Legality: the weights are synthetic-only, so they contain no window data [23]. The output could only feed a quota prior written by the captain (legal route). s2 is frozen.
- Licence: TabPFN-2.6 is non-commercial and gated; TabICLv2 not checked.
- Counted backtest: none (composition confound).
- Expected gain: 0 (unknown). The zebrafish result needed about 70 rows per fold; we have about 5 stage rows from two sampling regimes, so the model would return whatever prior it is given.
- Repeats a prune: yes ("hierarchical composition meta-model with scANVI", V3). New: a learner free of contamination, but no new information.

**6. Temporal priors across studies and cell types: maturation clock [6], DCI [5], Gene-Chronos [12], Sunbear [11].**
