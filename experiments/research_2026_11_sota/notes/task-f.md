## Findings
1. The 2025 Virtual Cell Challenge (H1 hESC, 300 CRISPRi perturbations, ~300,000 cells; >5,000 registrants, >1,200 teams submitting, >300 final submissions) was won by hybrids tuned to the metrics: 1st BM_xTVC (scFoundation-derived network, protein-model embeddings, training-set DEG-frequency and mean-expression features, a fused PDS + DES + small-weight MAE loss, pseudobulk training and outputs repeated "to meet DES requirements"), 2nd XLearning Lab (pseudobulk MLP with ESM-2 embeddings and residual delta learning, aimed at PDS, which the wrap-up says carried about twice DES's weight), 3rd Outlier (TransPert: summary-statistics transfer from similar reference cell lines plus a CV-tuned global linear scale for PDS, public data only) [1].
2. The 2025 aggregate scored each metric against a cell-mean baseline and clipped below-baseline contributions to zero, so MAE dropped out: almost all models were worse than the baseline on MAE, the winner's MAE was more than 37 times the baseline's (yet the lowest of the top 10), and without the clipping all top-50 entries would have scored below zero overall [1][5].
3. L1/L2 perturbation discrimination rises with the size of the predicted effect and plateaus at a sign-cosine limit; with norm matching, K562 mean effects transferred to H1 (115 perturbations, 7,581 genes) scored ~0.5 (chance) on L1/L2 PDS but ~0.8 on cosine PDS, so part of the top PDS scores was scale rather than biology [4][1].
4. On the Wilcoxon-based DES, scores plateaued near 40% while a technical replicate scored about 39%, and mismatched normalisation between control and prediction inflated DE calls until cell-eval was patched to block it [5][7].
5. Simple pseudobulk statistics sat close to the winners: a regression "first described in 1970" led the live board for a while and finished 15th (of 337 teams by Turbine's listing; 3rd on the generalist board), a pseudobulk kernel ridge regression with a cross-dataset relatedness kernel finished 38th of >1,200 entries (1st on Pearson), and on the released 100-perturbation TEST split a deterministic centered-transfer method scored 0.3413 against 0.3401 for the winning archive entry, trading +0.023 DES for -0.011 PDS with zero MAE contribution for both [6][5][9].
6. The organisers' stated lessons are that "no single metric captures model quality" and that a narrow scoring function "invites optimization against the metric rather than the biology", so VCC 2026 (zero-shot; validation live 2026-08-20, final test 2026-10-22, final submissions due 2026-11-05, winners mid-late November 2026) ranks on an unweighted, reference-normalised average of six metrics (one expression, one discrimination, four DE) over three held-out cell lines, with 0 = the cell-context mean and negative values allowed [11][10][12]. [spot-check: not verifiable for the scoring details; the rest verified. [11] confirms validation live 2026-08-20, final test 2026-10-22, final submissions due 2026-11-05, winners "mid-late November", "an aggregate across a broader panel of six metrics", three cell lines held back for final testing, and both quoted lessons. The metric families, the unweighted mean, the reference normalisation (0 = the cell-context mean) and the negatives could not be checked: [12] renders no text without JavaScript, and [10]'s PubMed abstract states no scoring rule.]
7. In the 2022 Kaggle multimodal competition (1,602 competitors, >27,000 submissions; private test = a later day for all four donors: day 10 for Multiome, day 7 for CITE-seq), the winners beat a kNN baseline by only +0.06 Pearson on Multiome (0.58 vs 0.52) and +0.03 on CITE-seq (0.85 vs 0.82) and the 2021 best by +0.02, using ensembles, several preprocessings, per-target feature selection, neural correction of simpler models and validation on unseen days or donors, while PPI, gene-activity, enhancer and eQTL priors did not help [13].
8. In the same competition the public-test score was the best predictor of private rank across 23 model variants (Spearman 0.944), adversarial validation came next (0.910), held-out-day and held-out-donor schemes ranged 0.583-0.867, one solution fell from 2nd on the public board to 133rd privately, and a bootstrap (300 resamples of the top 50) found only each task's top rank robust [13]. [spot-check: verified on [13]: every quoted Spearman value, the 2nd -> 133rd fall and the bootstrap; the combined day-4-plus-D3m holdout (0.879) is not quoted.]
9. In the 2021 NeurIPS competition (172 teams, >2,600 models), simple methods won key subtasks (RBF kernel ridge on truncated SVD for GEX->ADT; a linear reduction for online CITE-seq embedding), most modality-prediction entries performed similarly, only 14 of 25 joint-embedding teams beat the baseline, and re-running the overall winner four times moved it by up to 8 ranks [14]. [spot-check: corrected: [14] says most solutions "performed similarly" and reports no test, so "statistically similar" is dropped; the baseline that 14 of 25 teams beat is named MNN in the text and "LMDS-based" only in the Figure 3 caption, so it is no longer called LMDS. Verified: 172 teams and >2,600 models, the GEX->ADT kernel ridge on truncated SVD (300/70 components, length scale 10, alpha 0.2), the simple linear reduction that won online CITE-seq embedding, and the four re-runs that moved the overall winner (DANCE) by up to 8 ranks.]
10. The 2023 Kaggle single-cell perturbations top-4 write-ups (about 1,097 teams; MRRMSE on DE statistics for 255 held-out cell-type x compound rows) used target encodings by cell type and compound, small sequence or tabular nets, mixed losses (1st: 0.32 MSE + 0.24 MAE + 0.24 LogCosh + 0.2 BCE), pseudo-labelling, median ensembling, clipping to the training range and truncated-SVD target denoising; the 4th-place team found random k-fold CV inadequate, and the organisers scheduled two "Top Shake-up Benefactors" talks [16][17][15][29].
11. Metric choice decides winners elsewhere too: on the 2026 VCPI DRUG-seq set (14,026 training compounds, scaffold split) ridge on fingerprints wins under a per-gene inverse-variance proxy but is the worst chemistry-aware model under the official active-set wMSE, where a fusion model beats it by 0.012 (p < 1e-4) [22], and control-referenced deltas with unweighted errors reward predicting the mean whenever the control is biased or the signal sparse [23].
12. The Broad Obesity challenge (Crunch 1 2025-12-08 to 2026-02-28, Crunch 2 to 2026-04-13) scored MMD, Pearson delta and an L1 on four program proportions on separate leaderboards with 100 predicted cells per perturbation; on the L1 even ranks 8-10 (Crunch 1) and 9-10 (Crunch 2) did not beat the baseline, and the Part 1 runner-up on MMD and L1 rendered cells by resampling real within-perturbation residuals around a ridge-predicted centroid (Gaussian noise was "rejected by MMD"), validated on perturbation-disjoint folds with verbatim copies of the metric code [20][21].
13. In the Broad autoimmune spatial challenge (~1,000 participants, 62 countries), the organisers said all ranked entries "significantly outperform the baseline" but differ only modestly from each other, and the unseen-gene task was won by similarity-based cross-modal expression transfer [19].
14. On final picks, the record shows that score-chasing fails, not that boldness pays: M5 teams that tuned "magic multipliers" (x1.02-1.05) to the public board ranked poorly privately while robust-CV teams rose 1,000-5,000 places [24] [spot-check: verified on [24] section 3.3.2: multipliers "e.g., ×1.02–1.05"; the risers, "1 000 - 5 000 places up", are the teams "who avoided these tweaks", and robust models "relying on cross-validation without magic numbers generalized better"], a BirdCLEF+ 2025 note reports top public entries converging into a "public-kernel monoculture" and kept a decorrelated second pick while its primary was its best private score of 40 [25], and Turbine's model led VCC's 50-perturbation live board yet finished 15th on the 100-perturbation final set [6].

## Deep Read Notes
### Source [1]: Arc VCC 2025 wrap-up
Key data: winners' methods as in finding 1; MAE almost always worse than the baseline, so it "stopped influencing" the contest; PDS rewards patterns more than magnitudes and rises with magnitude (citing [4]); the generalist prize went to the best mean rank over 7 metrics among the 50 top finalists (Altos: gene-space flow matching pretrained on ~7M cells).
Key insight: the separating skill was metric optimisation ("ML specialists") on an exploitable aggregate; the organisers added a prize rather than change the rules mid-contest.
Useful for: VEC closes these exploits (rank-based DE, partial correlation, no zero-clip, control rows), so VCC's winning edge does not transfer; its pseudobulk-first, transfer-based core matches ours already.
### Source [13]: Lance et al. 2026, post-mortem of the 2022 Kaggle multimodal competition (read about 100k of 166k characters, then a targeted pass of the rest)
Key data: splits in finding 7; winners vs kNN and 2021 best; Spearman of validation schemes to private ranking: public 0.944, adversarial 0.910, random 0.896/0.889, held-out day 4 0.867, donor D3m 0.850, day 2 0.716, donor D2m 0.583; one solution 2nd -> 133rd; 7 of 12 gold-medal solutions kept their level; the paper is internally inconsistent on that solution's public rank.
Key insight: under a time shift, a single held-out-time validation ranks models only moderately (0.72-0.87); priors did not help; the margins that decided medals were 0.01-0.06 Pearson.
Useful for: calibrating trust in our time-shift backtests (both counted backtests and 10 paired seeds are the right defence) and expecting the VEC top-3 margins to be small and noisy.
### Source [14]: Lance et al. 2022, NeurIPS 2021 lessons learned
Key data: 172 teams, >2,600 models (1,393 in modality prediction); kernel ridge on truncated SVD (300/70 components, length scale 10, alpha 0.2) won GEX->ADT; four re-runs of the overall winner moved it by up to 8 ranks; 14 of 25 teams beat the joint-embedding baseline (MNN in the text, "LMDS-based" in a figure caption; corrected in audit); RMSE rewarded predicting zeros on sparse ATAC.
Key insight: when entries cluster, ranks are partly noise, simple kernels win small-data tasks, and a sparse-target metric invites zero-heavy predictions.
Useful for: the seed-noise and best-of-two arithmetic in the levers; VEC's variogram and mmd_u already penalise zero-heavy or low-variance outputs.
### Source [9]: Luan et al. 2026, centered transfer on the VCC TEST100 split
Key data: deterministic transfer (public summaries, a DE-priority kernel fitted on H1 training targets, sign and magnitude-rank transfer from a KOLF2.1J source, isotonic rendering) 0.341344 vs the BM_xTVC archive 0.340052; DES 0.3718 (+0.023), PDS 0.8614 (-0.011); reconstructed anchors bD 0.09505, bP 0.5082, bM 0.02496; four frozen versions were used in sequence on TEST, so it is not a single blind test.
Key insight: the winner's composite is reachable without learning, by trading DES against PDS; "repeated mean profiles can score well without representing single-cell variability".
Useful for: evidence that a statistical rank-transfer core is competitive; VEC's mmd_u and variogram would punish mean-only outputs that VCC's DES/PDS rewarded.
### Source [5]: LaPierre, participant post-mortem
Key data: pseudobulk kernel ridge 38th of >1,200 entries, 10th generalist, 1st on Pearson; the winner's MAE >37x the baseline's; DE ~40% vs a technical replicate ~39%; without clipping all top 50 negative; only cross-dataset correlations of perturbation effects helped as features; hard perturbations were the dataset-idiosyncratic ones.
Key insight: the author calls the clipping "the biggest mistake"; the field converged on pseudobulk linear or shallow models.
Useful for: the frontier was a shared statistical core plus metric handling, so VEC ranks may hinge on idiosyncratic target features and luck rather than method class.
### Source [4]: Liu et al. 2025, PDS distance and scale (Team Outlier)
Key data: L1/L2 PDS rises with a global scale factor and plateaus (no numeric optimum given); norm-matched PDS ~0.5 for K562->hESC mean effects; cosine PDS ~0.8; per-10k vs median normalisation changes L1/L2 norms markedly.
Key insight: the third-place team both used a CV-tuned global scale and documented the artefact; they recommend cosine or norm-matched PDS.
Useful for: in VEC the only scale-sensitive DE-family metric is T3's severity_slope; de_score and de_direction are rank-based and immune.

## SOTA-to-VEC mapping
1. **Metric-exploiting post-processing from VCC 2025**: global scaling for PDS [1][4], outputs repeated for DES [1][9], MAE given up under the zero-clip [1][5], normalisation mismatch [7], variance inflation or log1p transforms [8].
   - What it does: raises a composite without better biology. Evidence: Outlier placed 3rd with a CV-tuned scale; the winner's MAE was 37x the baseline's; [8] claims transformed random data beat top models (unverified).
   - VEC board and group: no DE group moves. de_score takes the prediction's top n_up / bottom n_dn genes by dp with n fixed by the truth and a magnitude null; de_direction is a rank partial correlation; DE calls use truth cells only. The skill map has no zero-clip (a metric worse than its floor gets skill < 0.5), so trading mmd_u or variogram for DE loses points. The only DE-family scale lever is T3's |severity| (weight .25).
   - Legality: a scale tuned on the cross-knockout folds is tuned on counted folds, so it cannot pass the adoption rule; otherwise legal. Licence: ideas only. [legality corrected in audit: not simply "otherwise legal". VEC's one such lever, a T3 magnitude scale, changes the pre-registered T3 rules, which move only by the captain's ruling, and no test score may set it (a test score is never evidence).]
   - Counted backtest: T3 cross-knockout folds (already spent by K2a/K2b and the frozen rules).
   - Expected gain: T3's magnitude group has 2.68 / 1.03 points left on the stand-ins (TRIES headroom), so at most ~1-2.7 T3 points with an equal downside on overshoot: 0 to +0.5; T1 and T2 <= 0.
   - Pruned already: "a two-scenario factor exponent", shrinkage, and the T1 bold s2 with an exaggerated Feng step (T1 is flat in step size). New: only outside evidence that CV-tuned scale helps on scale-sensitive metrics [4] and that score-tuned multipliers fail [24].
2. **Pseudobulk-first prediction rendered with real cells** (XLearning, Outlier, Turbine, LaPierre [1][5][6]; the Broad Obesity runner-up's real-residual resampling around a predicted centroid, with optional re-centring [21]).

   - VEC board and group: T1 distribution and covariance, T3 mutant state, T2 cell state. This is already our design (real E10.5, WT or bracket cells with count-space shifts).
   - Legality: legal (released data). Licence: code private; ideas only. Counted backtest: T1 E10.5 from E9.5 and from E8.5.
   - Expected gain: 0 for the core (in place). The re-centring step is the pruned exact top-up: 0.1-0.4 expected on T1, judged through the composition confound.
   - Pruned already: "the same exact top-up on T1", b0_smooth, NB resampling, latent Gaussian-OT decoding. New: outside confirmation that real-residual rendering beats synthetic noise on an MMD leaderboard, which argues against any decoded or synthetic cells.
