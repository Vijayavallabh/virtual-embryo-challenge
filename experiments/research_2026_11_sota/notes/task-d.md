## Findings
1. ContextFlow (arXiv v3, May 2026) is the clearest 2026 held-out-time-point test on spatial omics, but it generates expression only (coordinates feed its 2D priors), and its margin over the best flow-matching baseline on the held-out middle stage is about 10% weighted W2 on axolotl brain (2.316 vs 2.567, next-step sampling; Metric-FM still wins MMD, 0.029 vs 0.030) and 5% W2 on liver regeneration (32.68 vs 34.30), with lambda and alpha picked per table and no copy-nearest, identity or linear-interpolation baseline [1].
2. NicheFlow (NeurIPS 2025) generates 2D niches (coordinates plus 50-PC states) from the previous slide, is tested in-distribution only with no held-out stage and no copy baseline, and its gain over the same model on random point clouds comes from radius-niche sampling (1NN-F1 0.628 vs 0.554 on axolotl brain, 0.285 vs 0.271 on mouse brain ageing); it is neither rotation- nor translation-invariant [2].
3. stVCR (Nature Methods, March 2026) learns one rigid transform per time point (three Euler angles plus a translation in 3D) jointly with unbalanced dynamical OT and spatial-velocity priors, and is checked by leave-one-time-point-out interpolation against TIGON, SF2M and static OT with its numbers only in supplementary tables and no copy-nearest baseline; its 3D Drosophila application has two time points [3].
4. TP-DATE (arXiv, September 2026) turns Gromov-Wasserstein OT into flow matching and predicts a held-out intermediate slice better than OT-CFM, GW- and FGW-CFM, stVCR, CytoBridge and ContextFlow on most metrics, yet stVCR keeps the better spatial W2 on axolotl (0.0858 vs 0.0920) and ContextFlow on a 3D serial-section tumour stack (0.0677 vs 0.0696), and no static FGW-plus-McCann or copy baseline is run [4]. [spot-check: corrected: the baseline list, the 5 seeds and the four spatial W2 figures match, and no copy-nearest or identity baseline is run. But a static FGW baseline is run, in learned form: FGW-CFM computes one FGW-OT coupling between the two snapshots (OT and GW-OT fused 7:3) and trains a velocity network on straight-line (displacement) conditional paths (Appendix B.7), "to demonstrate the necessity of dynamic QOT against static QOT". TP-DATE beats it on spatial W2 (axolotl 0.0920 vs 0.1000; tumour 0.0696 vs 0.0746), and on the tumour plain OT-CFM (0.0704) also beats FGW-CFM. No paper found runs the closed-form version on real cells, which is our s1's class. ContextFlow also edges TP-DATE on axolotl spatial W2 (0.0917 vs 0.0920). The paper does not discuss GW's mirror ambiguity.]
5. Mimyr (bioRxiv November 2025; ICLR 2026) is the only 2025-2026 generator found that reports a copy-nearest ("lookup") baseline: on held-out MERSCOPE brain slices it lifts soft Spearman from 0.39/0.44/0.48 to 0.45/0.53/0.58 and soft F1 from 0.65 to 0.70 after training on about 10M cells, and the held-out unit is a slice, not a stage [8]. [spot-check: verified: lookup definition, the three soft Spearman pairs, soft F1 0.65 -> 0.70 (averages), mouse-brain MERSCOPE data and no held-out time point all match. Precision: the paper states no training-cell count; it trains on all sections except nine held-out slices (four validation, five test) of an atlas of "approximately ten million cells", so "about 10M" is the atlas size and a close upper bound.]
6. SOCS (bioRxiv 2025; Nature Communications 2026) adds a structure Gromov-Wasserstein term that keeps contiguous structures together, [removed: W — a cited method's benchmark results on data that may fall in a protected window], but it predicts no held-out time point and assumes nearly every cell has a partner [5].
7. Section-interpolation methods return expression fields at given coordinates rather than cells: SINTER3D (Genome Biology, June 2026) beats Spateo's interpolators on the held-out even sections of a 35-section mouse brain (cosine 0.85 vs 0.79, Pearson 0.81 vs 0.71) and absorbs per-section effects with a learnable section embedding and offset, with no nearest-section baseline [6].
8. isoST (bioRxiv, August 2025) pushes cells through small depth steps with SDEs to reach isotropic 3D resolution, was benchmarked by holding out 26 of 53 interleaved MERFISH brain slices against Sparse VFC, a Gaussian process and an MLP (values only in figures), and its authors state it cannot predict structures absent from the sampled input [7].
9. The morphodynamic models of the gastrulation-to-heart-tube interval are built on embryos inside the embryo window: Raiola et al. (eLife, July 2026) derive growth rate, anisotropy and strain on myocardial meshes from 16 live-imaged embryos staged E7.75-E8.25, with machine-learning morphometric staging into nine atlas stages, validate motion against more than 9,000 tracked cells, and report no held-out shape-prediction error [13].
10. Continuous morphological staging exists but cannot place a hidden sample and is barely legal: the 4D OPT atlas of Wong et al. (2015) stages embryos at 72-minute resolution from 51 interpolated volumes but spans E11.5-E14.0 (barred), and the DMDD E14.5 HREM embryos fall into six forelimb sub-stages from S21 to S23 (Geyer et al. 2017), of which on our reading only S23- and S23 pass the team's Theiler midpoint rule [16][17]. [spot-check: verified for [17]: six sub-stages S21, S22-, S22, S22+, S23-, S23, scored on forelimb (handplate) appearance alone. The paper classifies its controls as TS21, TS22 and TS23 and describes the handplate system for embryos within Theiler stages 22 and 23; in what was read it gives no sub-stage-by-sub-stage Theiler table, so "S23- and S23 = TS23" stays our reading, consistent with that description.]
11. Legal whole-heart 3D morphology after E13.5 exists as DMDD HREM (over 200 wild-type C57BL/6N E14.5 embryos, 2.5-3.5 µm isotropic voxels), an E15.5 micro-CT average of 35 C57BL/6J embryos at 28 µm with 48 labelled structures, and eMouseAtlas MRI models at TS23-TS24, but each is an organ volume rather than a heart-region cell cloud, and heart labels in the last two are unverified [17][18][19][20]. [spot-check: verified for the DMDD part through [17]: "All 215 wildtype embryos were staged" (C57BL/6N), with HREM data resampled to 3 x 3 x 3 µm³ voxels. The same programme's E14.5 images also cover 58 embryonic-lethal null mutant lines [17], so a wild-type filter is needed. The article (CC BY) has no data-availability statement, so DMDD's data terms remain unchecked.]
12. In our released heart MERFISH files the scored cloud is not the organ: at E9.5, 63% of cells are non-cardiac shell; every stage has 30-40 sections, so the stack cuts the heart; the full-cloud radius is non-monotone across stages because the imaged field changes; and shell removal or a core crop alone moves Dice to 0.86 and 0.79, so whole-organ morphology could set at most the core sub-cloud's shape [29].
13. The data-generating lab assembles serial MERFISH sections in 3D with Spateo's non-rigid alignment (MERFISH+ human heart: 53 sections at 112 µm), our released files store z as section index times a per-file step of 10-15 with 97-98% of 3D nearest neighbours in the same section, and a 1.5x z-factor mismatch costs 12.6-12.8 heart-board points on an otherwise perfect prediction, so isotropic resampling (isoST, SINTER3D, UniST) would harm the scored geometry [27][29][7][6][10].
14. Legal spatial atlases at the embryo brackets are thin: the SEU-3D whole-embryo Stereo-seq digital embryos (Cell 2025) are legal only at E8.0, the Harland et al. gastrulation atlas adds sections from E6.5 to E7.5 of which only stages at or before E7.25 are legal, and the 3D Slide-seq embryos of Sampath Kumar et al. (2023) cover E8.5-E9.5, whose E8.5 lies inside the heart-interpolation window and cannot enter the shared core [21][22][23].
15. Flysta3D-v2 (Cell 2025) offers 3D single-cell Stereo-seq of 43 Drosophila embryos collected every 0.5-2 h from serial 7-8 µm sagittal sections: a window-free, bilaterally symmetric serial-section time series dense enough for many t = 1/2 interpolation triples [26]. [spot-check: verified on the resource page: 43 embryo samples (plus 9 larva and 5 pupa samples), embryos at "0.5 to 2 h intervals throughout embryogenesis", Stereo-seq on "sagittal sections of 7 or 8 μm thickness", single-cell spatial transcriptomes (3,812,505 cell bins) with scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq layers. The page names no journal or year, so "Cell 2025" is unchecked, and it states no data licence, only CNGB's terms and conditions.]

## Deep Read Notes
### Source [1]: ContextFlow
Key data: held-out middle (interpolation) and last (extrapolation) time points; 10 runs; baselines CFM, MOTFM, Metric-FM, MFM. Axolotl interpolation weighted W2 2.316 vs 2.567 (next step) and 3.465 vs 4.198 (IVP); extrapolation IVP 5.277 vs 6.503, but next-step MFM 6.868 beats ContextFlow's 6.914; liver W2 32.68 vs 34.30.
Key insight: spatial-smoothness and ligand-receptor (LIANA+) priors buy 5-20% over learned flow baselines, with per-table selection of lambda and alpha; expression only, 2D; the authors list missing ground-truth validation as a limitation.
Useful for: calibrating what "SOTA" buys at a held-out stage: no published gain over a copy of the nearest stage. Its organogenesis rows use in-window stages and are not recorded here.

### Source [2]: NicheFlow
Key data: Table 1, 5 runs, lambda 0.1. Axolotl brain (ABD) 1NN-F1: NicheFlow GLVFM 0.628, RPCFlow 0.554, SPFlow 0.190; SPD 0.576 vs 1.038. Mouse brain ageing (MBA): 0.285, 0.271, 0.205. On PSD the random-cloud variant is slightly better on both (2.051 vs 2.079; 1.543 vs 1.554).
Key insight: the niche-as-point-cloud sampling carries the gain; the objective (GLVFM vs CFM) adds 0.002-0.024. In-distribution only; needs pre-aligned, normalised slides; moscot is compared only qualitatively; open-source release "anticipated" in the paper.
Useful for: Q3. No validated unseen-stage niche generator exists, and the 15-NN metric is content-dominated [30], so niche arrangement can move little.

### Source [4]: TP-DATE (dynamic GW flow matching)
Key data: hold-one-out intermediate slice; baselines OT-CFM, GW-CFM, FGW-CFM, stVCR, CytoBridge, ContextFlow; 5 seeds. Axolotl (ARTISTA): spatial W2 0.0920 (stVCR 0.0858), expression W2 5.39, fused W2 8.01. 3D tumour, depth treated as time: spatial W2 0.0696 (ContextFlow 0.0677). [audit: also FGW-CFM 0.1000 (axolotl) and 0.0746 (tumour), OT-CFM 0.1014 and 0.0704, GW-CFM 0.1077 and 0.0773, ContextFlow 0.0917 (axolotl), stVCR 0.0706 (tumour), all spatial W2.]
Key insight: the newest GW-based dynamic method neither addresses GW's mirror ambiguity nor compares against static FGW plus displacement interpolation, which is our s1's class. [corrected in audit: it does compare against a static FGW coupling with displacement paths, in learned form (FGW-CFM, OT:GW 7:3, a velocity network trained on the coupling's straight-line paths), and beats it on spatial W2 by about 7-8%. It runs no closed-form, real-cell version (our s1) and no copy. The mirror point stands.]
Useful for: no evidence that 2026 dynamic GW beats our fgw entry; any trial would start untested. [corrected in audit: only indirect evidence: on two non-mouse held-out-slice tasks, dynamic GW beats a learned static-FGW flow, while stVCR and ContextFlow beat TP-DATE on the same spatial metric. Nothing tests real cells moved along a static coupling, or a copy, so a trial would still start untested.] Its MOSTA mouse-brain rows are not recorded (stages not checked).

### Source [8]: Mimyr
Key data: lookup baseline (positions from the nearest reference slice, cell types by 20-NN vote, expression copied from the closest same-type cell). Held-out atlas slices soft Spearman 0.39/0.44/0.48 -> 0.45/0.53/0.58; soft F1 0.65 -> 0.70; zero-shot cross-panel 0.48/0.52 -> 0.56/0.59. The 23M-parameter model was trained on about 10M MERSCOPE cells and is conditioned on a plane descriptor (a point and a normal). [audit: the training-cell count is not stated: training uses all sections except nine held-out slices of an atlas of about ten million cells; the 23M parameters are the medium model's (a small one has 0.25M).]
Key insight: a copy-nearest baseline is strong. Ten million training cells buy +0.06 to +0.10 soft Spearman across a spatial gap; no temporal gap is tested; density and neighbourhood metrics are not reported.
Useful for: a prior that learned generation trained on four released embryo stages would not beat real-cell copies.

### Source [6]: SINTER3D
Key data: odd/even split of 35 coronal mouse-brain sections; against Spateo VTK/SVFC: cosine 0.85 vs 0.79, Pearson 0.81 vs 0.71, Spearman 0.64 vs 0.60-0.61. DLPFC interpolation ARI 0.62/0.63 against 0.70/0.67 on the real sections. Section embedding e_s and offset u_s; separate XY and Z multi-scale encoders; ICP alignment by default (PASTE or CAST optional).
Key insight: modelling section-level nuisance terms is now standard for serial sections; the output is a continuous field, not cells.
Useful for: Q5. It confirms per-section modelling, but it is not a geometry generator; the authors flag rising uncertainty with wide spacing and large morphological change between sections.

### Source [13]: Raiola et al. 2026 (heart-tube deformation)
Key data: 16 embryos (11 analysed), MIRT non-rigid frame-to-frame registration, "Live-Shapes" meshes registered to the Esteban 2022 atlas, morphometric staging into nine stages; outputs are growth (area ratio J), anisotropy, maximum strain and a strain-agreement index. Motion was checked against more than 9,000 tracked cells [removed: W — a validation outcome measured on embryos inside a protected window]. Code: [removed in audit: W — a code-repository name that may repeat the title wording removed by the screen; the code is linked from the paper's page]; data on Mendeley.
Key insight: growth-tensor fields are validated for motion, not for forecasting shape, and only two specimens were averaged at the late stages.
Useful for: a method template only (deformation transfer onto a core sub-cloud). The data lie in the embryo window, and the developmental results were not read.

## SOTA-to-VEC mapping
Counting backtests throughout: embryo = E7.5 (t = 1/3) and E7.25 (t = 2/3); heart = E10.5 from E9.5 and from <= E8.75 (brief section 4). Board points convert to Overall at x0.5 on both T2 boards.

**(a) Learned spatiotemporal flows: ContextFlow [1], stVCR [3], TP-DATE [4]; niches: NicheFlow [2]**
- What: they learn a velocity field between consecutive snapshots (OT-coupled flow matching; unbalanced dynamical OT with a learned rigid frame per stage; dynamic GW). ContextFlow moves expression only, NicheFlow 2D niches, stVCR and TP-DATE expression and coordinates.
- Evidence: held-out middle stages in axolotl, liver and a 3D tumour stack. Every baseline is another learned flow; margins are 5-20% in W2 and mixed (TP-DATE loses spatial W2 to stVCR and ContextFlow). There is no copy-nearest and no static FGW-plus-McCann baseline, and NicheFlow is in-distribution only. [corrected in audit: TP-DATE's FGW-CFM is a static FGW coupling with displacement paths, fitted by a learned flow, and TP-DATE beats it on spatial W2 (0.0920 vs 0.1000 axolotl; 0.0696 vs 0.0746 tumour) [4]. What is missing is the closed-form version on real cells (our s1's class) and any copy-nearest baseline.]

Our E8 already showed blended or averaged cells are "too narrow a population" (mmd_u 0.671 -> 0.638 on E7.5, ahead in 0 of 10) [30].
