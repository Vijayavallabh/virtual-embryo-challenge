## Findings
1. The only 2025 to November 2026 source I found that scores a forecaster against a naive copy-last baseline on held-out time points at 2,000 genes is scFM (arXiv, May 2026, venue unconfirmed): extrapolation W2 (Sinkhorn, on decoded 2,000-HVG log expression) was 599.98 and 653.19 against copy-last 887.69 and 943.98 on a zebrafish embryo series (t = 10, 11), 529.69 and 682.66 against 824.57 and 969.15 on Drosophila embryos (t = 9, 10), and 112.14, 112.76 and 121.42 against 161.35, 169.72 and 167.25 on mouse fibroblast reprogramming (t = 16 to 18, 12-h steps), i.e. 27-36% below copy-last, with the 2024 scNODE close behind (607.87 against 887.69 at zebrafish t = 10) [1][24].
2. Those margins are the size that denoising alone produces (my derivation, not the authors'): in high-dimensional empirical W2 a mean-like decoded output costs about sigma*sqrt(d) against real cells while a real-cell copy costs about sqrt(2)*sigma*sqrt(d), a ratio near 0.71 against the observed 0.64-0.73, and copy-last's W2 grows little with horizon (+6% zebrafish, +18% Drosophila, +4% over two reprogramming steps), so the published gain is mostly noise removal, which VEC's variogram punishes (the organisers' pseudobulk_shift baseline falls below copy_last on T1 with variogram skill 38.9, about 2.2 points lost) [1][29].
3. Every other 2025-2026 forecaster I found reports gains only over learned models or a mean-shift baseline: CellTempo (18,791 protein-coding genes, NB decoder) beats a delta extrapolation by pseudotime PCC 0.74 +/- 0.35 against 0.63 +/- 0.33 on pseudotime-built trajectories (not real held-out days), LGP-OT beats the best learned baseline by at most 2% W2 on Drosophila extrapolation (32.15, 32.52, 36.51 against 32.80, 32.88, 37.06), and PACE (NeurIPS 2026) reports 23.7% lower MMD/W1/W2 than the strongest competitor on low-dimensional held-out snapshots [3][4][23].
4. scTimeBench (bioRxiv March 2026; Bioinformatics ISMB issue August 2026) runs 10 methods (8 forecasting, 2 OT) on 8 datasets from 4 species but forecasts one step ahead only, reports mean ranks instead of effect sizes and has no copy-last or pseudobulk baseline for forecasting; scIMF ranks first and scNODE second, Squidiff is first on Gaussian MMD (mean rank 1.0) yet last on the other metrics, and in lineage recovery most methods "often underperform compared to a correlation baseline" [2][25].
5. No 2025-2026 source compares per-cell-type pseudobulk trend models with shrinkage against deep generative forecasters on held-out developmental time, and the Theis group's 2026 review still lists evaluation "beyond interpolation" as an open challenge [2][26].
6. Within one protocol, small tabular regressors forecast cell-type composition at strictly held-out stages: on zebrafish zscape (18-72 hpf, 98 cell types, 28 perturbations, about 70 training rows per fold) TabPFN reached Spearman 0.808 and R2 0.704 and CatBoost 0.763 and 0.662, against a mean baseline at R2 0.352 that falls to -0.49 on the backward 18 hpf fold; no copy-last or nearest-stage baseline was run [16]. [audit spot-check: verified against [16]: 18-72 hpf (five time points), about 2.7M cells, 98 types, 28 perturbations, 5-fold strict time-point hold-out; TabPFN 0.808/0.704, CatBoost 0.763/0.662, TabICL 0.741/0.604, Prophet 0.533/0.651; mean baseline R2 0.352, and -0.49 at 18 hpf; TabPFN and CatBoost not significantly different; the only baselines are a mean and a bootstrap baseline. 7. Pooled pseudobulk temporal models transfer across studies while single-study ones do not: a cell-type-agnostic elastic net on 905 per-type pseudobulks (462 of 10,957 genes kept) predicts human fetal brain age with a 2.65-week median error on held-out studies [removed: W — a held-out evaluation figure on further datasets whose species and stages are not established], a single-study model goes from 1.52 weeks inside its study to 8.03 weeks outside it, and cell-type-specific models are no better on their own type (3.94 weeks) than on other types (3.92 weeks, P = 0.25) [17]. 8. Mixed depth and culture effects break that transfer: on a 36-dataset organoid atlas with heterogeneous read depths even the rank-normalised model reached only R = 0.52 (the authors also report an age plateau after about six months in culture) [17]. [audit spot-check: verified against [17]: 36 datasets, a rank-normalised variant, overall R = 0.52; predicted age plateaus at about 5-6 months in cortical organoids and declines after about 6 months in the atlas.]
9. Temporal trends transfer to unseen cell types only for genes whose direction is consistent across types: in a cross-cell-type split of human trauma immune cells (four time points), a GRU reached MASE 0.78, about 22% below naive carry-forward, for genes with a dynamic consistency index above 0.8, no model beat carry-forward (MASE > 1) below 0.2, and the index computed on training types alone tracked the full index (Spearman 0.923) [18]. 10. Unbalanced transport in 2026 still sets growth from the coupling between two observed snapshots (WFR-FM regresses d/dt ln m_t with m1 = gamma1/gamma0; MUST-FM adds a lineage mask), so it cannot set a rate past the last observed stage, and its held-out gains are interpolation-only and small: W1 0.298 against 0.301 (EMT), 37.221 against 37.386 (CITE-seq), 6.586 against 6.868 (mouse haematopoiesis) and a loss on embryoid bodies (10.157 against 10.075), in 10-50 dimensions with no naive baseline [5][6].
11. Growth without target-time data appears only as a prior, and no prior is validated on held-out times: scMTG weights cells by z-scored proliferation and death gene-set scores (3,000 HVGs, interpolation only, median LISI +13.0% and SCC +4.3% over the second-best method on its Serum benchmark, no ablation of the growth term), ARTEMIS fits a kill rate to cell-count changes and wins 4 of 6 held-out interpolation columns against scNODE by at most 0.5 W, SUDO and USB report no held-out evaluation in their abstracts and DiffusionOT none in its full text [10][9][8][7][11].
12. None of these growth methods creates a population with no admissible source: couplings and lineage masks move or grow mass only along transitions present in the observed snapshots [5][6][8].
13. No 2025-2026 count-realistic generator is validated on held-out time points for sparsity or gene-gene covariance: count-FM (13,913 genes, birth-death jumps in count space) beats scDiffusion, scVI, scLDM and CFGen only on unconditional W2 (20.456 against 20.545-22.226) and MMD2 (0.0185 against 0.0202-0.0361) in a 2,000-HVG PCA and judges its P12-to-P35 transport by eye, and scLDM's NB variant reports Tabula Muris W2 7.267 against CFGen 11.658 and scDiffusion 14.143 with no held-out time [13][14].
14. Outside biology, a matched seven-model, five-seed study found an autoregressive hurdle (two-part zero and level) model beating a conditional flow on 5 of 6 zero-inflated datasets, with the flow's zero-occurrence statistics varying by up to 62% across seeds, which is weak support for editing detection and level separately [15].

## Deep Read Notes
### Source [1]: scFM, copy-last comparison at 2,000 genes
Key data: 2,000 HVGs (library-size normalised, log1p), 50-d VAE latent; zebrafish embryo (12 points), Drosophila (11, hourly), Schiebinger reprogramming (19, 12-h steps); one model per dataset, 3 interpolation points and the last 2-3 points held out; W2 (Sinkhorn) and mean pairwise L2 on decoded gene-space cells; scFM/copy-last W2 ratio 0.64-0.73 in extrapolation, 0.55 for zebrafish interpolation at t = 4 (426.58 against 775.63). No ablation, no code, no composition or sparsity analysis.
Key insight: the field's only copy-last comparison at 2,000 or more genes; the extrapolation margin is the size denoising buys in high-dimensional W2, and the ratio drifts toward 0.71 as the horizon grows.
Useful for: the counter-claim against decoded generators on T1, and a reason to report a split-half ceiling beside copy-last in any external comparison.
### Source [2]: scTimeBench (bioRxiv 2026; Bioinformatics, ISMB 2026)
Key data: 10 methods, 8 datasets, 4 species; forecasting = t to t+1 in three hold-out settings; 2,000 HVGs for the zebrafish, Drosophila and MEF sets, whole transcriptome for human pancreas; W, Gaussian MMD, energy MMD and Hausdorff, aggregated as mean ranks; scIMF first, scNODE second, MIOFlow and PI-SDE close; Squidiff first on Gaussian MMD only; OT methods cannot forecast; lineage: OT best, most methods at or below a correlation baseline; pseudotime alignment helps lineage.
Key insight: no naive copy baseline and no effect sizes; the choice of metric reorders the field.
Useful for: question 2, since no 2026 benchmark shows any family beating copy-last; harness design.
### Source [17]: Venkatesan et al. 2026, cross-study transfer of a pooled temporal axis
Key data: 2.89M human fetal brain cells, 13 datasets, 151 donors; 905 per-type pseudobulks; elastic net keeps 462 of 10,957 genes; leave-study-out 2.65 weeks, held-out types 2.86; single-study models 1.52 weeks in study, 8.03 weeks outside; type-specific models 3.94 weeks on their own type against 3.92 on others; depth-mixed organoid atlas, rank-normalised, R = 0.52.
Key insight: temporal axes shared across types transfer when pooled across studies; a single study's trend carries that study's effects; depth heterogeneity costs most of the accuracy.
Useful for: Lever 1's design (pool, gate, depth-match) and its counter-claim (depth).
### Source [16]: Palla et al. 2026, tabular forecasting of composition at held-out stages
Key data: zscape 18-72 hpf, about 2.7M cells, 98 types, 28 perturbations; 5-fold strict stage holdout; TabPFN 0.808/0.704, CatBoost 0.763/0.662, TabICL 0.741/0.604, Prophet 0.533/0.651 (Spearman/R2), mean baseline R2 0.352 (-0.49 at 18 hpf); about 70 rows per fold; TabPFN and CatBoost not significantly different.
Key insight: within one protocol a small tabular model forecasts composition at an unseen stage; no copy-last comparison was made.
Useful for: Lever 5's counter-claim; TabPFN provenance [27].
### Source [5]: WFR-FM (ICLR 2026), how growth is set
Key data: growth target d/dt ln m_t from a static WFR semi-coupling of adjacent snapshots (m1 = gamma1/gamma0), regressed jointly with the velocity; held-out interpolation W1: EMT 0.298 (VGFM 0.301), embryoid bodies 10.157 (DeepRUOT 10.075, a loss), CITE-seq 37.221 (37.386), mouse haematopoiesis 6.586 (TIGON 6.868); 10-50 dimensions; no naive baseline.
Key insight: growth is identified only between two observed snapshots, and held-out gains stay within 4%.
Useful for: question 3; growth cannot be set past E10.5 without a legal later anchor, so the growth line stays closed.
### Source [18]: Shi et al. 2026, cross-type consistency predicts transferability
Key data: human trauma immune cells, per-type summaries at four points (control, <4 h, 24 h, 72 h); index = mean pairwise cosine of per-type log-change vectors; disjoint train, validation and test types; GRU with Gaussian NLL MASE 0.78 for index > 0.8 (about 22% below carry-forward), MLP and transformer about 0.85, all models MASE > 1 for index < 0.2; training-type index against full index Spearman 0.923.
Key insight: cross-type consistency, computable from training types alone, tells where trend transfer beats carry-forward.
Useful for: the target-free gene gate in Levers 1 and 2.

## SOTA-to-VEC mapping
### A. Decoded latent forecasters: scFM [1], scNODE [24], scIMF [25], LGP-OT [4], CellTempo [3], CellPace [12], PACE [23]
- What it does: encodes cells (2,000-3,000 HVGs; CellTempo 18,791 genes) into a latent space, learns a flow, ODE, SDE, Gaussian process or diffusion over time, and decodes new cells.
- Evidence: one copy-last comparison (scFM, W2 27-36% below copy-last at 1-3 steps past the last snapshot; horizons of hours in the zebrafish and Drosophila series, 0.5-1.5 days in reprogramming); all other comparisons are against learned models (2% or less for LGP-OT; 23.7% for PACE, low-dimensional) or a delta shift (CellTempo, overlapping SDs). The VEC horizon is 2 days of mouse organogenesis.
- Board and group: T1. A decoded set contracts within-type spread and fills zeros, so it would cost covariance (variogram, .20) and probably distribution (mmd_u, .30, whose 0.25x-bandwidth kernels penalise a contracted cloud). DE recovery and direction would come from what the model learns on E8.5, E9.5 and E10.5 alone, which is momentum, and momentum lost to Feng's bracket in a test-like geometry (sign 0.73 against 0.92, Spearman 0.40 against 0.80) [29].
- Legality: training from scratch on the released stages is legal (windows, genotype, KO ruling, post-exposure block, A15: no issue). CellTempo and Chreode weights plausibly contain E9.5-E13.5 mouse cells: barred unless provenance shows otherwise. CellPace is usable for its method only.
- Licence: scNODE MIT [29]; scFM publishes no code; CellTempo (GitHub EperLuo/CellTempo) and CellPace state no licence on the pages read.
- Counted backtest: E10.5 from E9.5 and from E8.5, but with one or two earlier stages the dynamics are under-identified, and the backtests' composition gap (inputs about 64% cardiomyocytes, about 19% in the chamber-cut truth) dominates mmd_u [30].
- Expected gain: negative, about -0.5 to -2.5 T1 points. The lower end is pseudobulk_shift's variogram loss (about 2.2 points [29]); nothing suggests the DE groups would beat the Feng bracket the s1 already uses.
- Repeats a pruned idea: yes, "T1: whole-ladder flow with growth" (2026-11-04) and "decoded sampling" and JL-OT (2026-11-05) [30]. New: the sqrt(2) reading of the published gains (Finding 2), which says the literature's best number is not evidence for us.
### B. Unbalanced (growth) transport: WFR-FM [5], MUST-FM [6], SUDO [8], USB [7], ARTEMIS [9], scMTG [10], DiffusionOT [11]
- What it does: learns a growth (birth-death) field with the velocity. Mass targets come from a semi-coupling of two observed snapshots, constrained by lineage masks (MUST-FM), convex or asymmetric penalties (SUDO), discrete jumps (USB), cell-count changes (ARTEMIS) or gene-set scores (scMTG).
- Evidence: interpolation only, in 100 dimensions or fewer except the HVG-based ARTEMIS and scMTG; WFR-FM held-out W1 gains of 1-4% with one loss; ARTEMIS within 0.5 W and 4 of 6; scMTG has no growth ablation; no naive baseline anywhere.
- Board and group: T1 proportions, hence distribution (mmd_u, .30) and, through composition, the DE groups.
- Legality: the post-exposure block lets T1 composition change only through quotas at the frozen count, from a prior the captain writes from legal data, never through weights. This family reweights cells by construction, so it is barred as a model. A growth rate for E10.5 to E12.5 would need a later legal anchor (Feng at E14.5 or later; Feulner only at a stage after its Theiler-stage-22 E14.5 sample and only after a register row with filter code; JAX at E14.25 or later, embryos 54-74) whose capture differs from the target's [legality corrected in audit: was "Feng or Feulner at E14.5 or later, JAX at E13.75 or later"; X006 keeps Feulner's TS22-coded E14.5 sample out and lets nothing from Feulner into a model; X003's legal late JAX set is embryos 54-74 (E14.25-P0), and embryos staged E13.75-E14.0 are excluded under the staging working answer (docs/ORGANIZER_QUESTIONS.md Q7)].
