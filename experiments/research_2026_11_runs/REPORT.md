## 2. Reference-sample and run effects: estimators we can implement

**Shared reference.** When several conditions are each contrasted with one reference measurement, their deviations correlate with no true effect, `Corr(d_a,d_b) = s_ref^2/sqrt((s_a^2+s_ref^2)(s_b^2+s_ref^2))`, 0.5 at equal variances; in the package's simulated example (12,000 units, 8 conditions, 90% of units null) ignoring this gave 2,932 false positives among 3,358 calls, against 1 among 58 [2][3]. Every knockout scored against one wild-type file thus shares minus that file's deviation, and knockouts scored against different files share none of it [unverified reading]; the 0.15 correlation between our two knockout factors [93][33] is compatible with either case and does not by itself show a shared term [counter-review]. Independent sampling noise pulls that figure down and a shared reference pushes it up, so it is neither a floor nor a ceiling until both are estimated [counter-review]. The noise part is removed by `rho = cov(d1,d2)/sqrt((var(d1)-mean(se1^2))(var(d2)-mean(se2^2)))`: identical perturbations in two screens correlated 0.16 on raw fold changes and 0.90 on noise-corrected impact [100].

**Regression to the mean.** With repeatability `R = g^2/(g^2+d^2)`, change regressed on baseline has slope R - 1 when nothing truly depends on baseline, and d^2 cannot be estimated from two measurements [7]; Blomqvist's corrected slope is `(b_c*V(x1)+d^2)/(V(x1)-d^2)` [7][54]. A scored delta is thus predicted from the reference's estimated deviation with slope -(1 - R) [unverified reading].

**In-run reference.** In bulk multi-omics with one reference material run in every batch, and each group in its own batch, mean-centring and ComBat failed while the per-feature log ratio to the reference measured in the same batch restored the signal, even with a single or a noisy reference replicate; SVA and RUV variants also held up there, and the method "assumes that there is no interaction between batch and the reference sample" [1]. Lineages the knockout cannot touch are our in-run reference, the form of s1's factor [unverified reading]; unlike the reference of [1] they are not one material measured in every run, so embryo and staging differences enter as the interaction the method assumes away [counter-review].

**False discoveries.** Ignoring between-sample variance manufactures differential expression, mostly in highly expressed genes [23]; with one plate per group every tested method lost error control [24]. Of 1,727 naive hits, 1,012 also differed between processing cohorts among controls [4], and 254 of 527 between experiments in identical reference samples [18]; batch adjustment in an unbalanced design gave 2,011 hits against 11 with batch blocked [25]. Recurring nuisance modules are histone, sex-linked and mitochondrial genes [95] and immediate-early, cell-cycle, matrix and immune clusters [101].

**Learning run variation.** RUV-III needs only replicate sets, and pseudo-samples resampled from one sample per group suffice [4][16]; per-cell-type factors controlled false discoveries where one shared factor per sample lost power [4]; theory exists [30]. CellANOVA learns a gene-space basis from a control pool, removes it by projection and tests the pool by leaving one control out; its run effects were "highly cell type specific" [6].

**Imaging runs.** The same array and panel a year apart gave per-gene Spearman 0.94-0.99 on three platforms while median level moved by -18%, 4-fold up and 8-fold down [10]; other replicate studies report correlations of 0.95-1.00 or strong per-gene similarity [12][13][14][15]. Readout reagents age with run duration and some readouts add tissue-specific false counts [11]; different genes were low in different serial sections, by cell type [5]. Corrections in use: a global scale [15], level-dependent normalisation [28], per-gene count mixed models [5], or one per-gene platform effect shared by cell types (R^2 0.90) [22], estimated between a sequencing reference and a spatial assay, not between two runs of one assay [counter-review: from the method's design as the counter-reviewer recalls it; the page was not re-opened].

**Mutant embryos.** Chimera studies compare mutant with host cells of the same embryos [111]; a 22-mutant atlas advises an in-line wild-type control because reused controls carry batch effects [102]. Cre alone caused defects in two of three alleles examined [112] and disturbed about 26% of 27 tested genes in adult hearts [117]; litter explains 30-60% of the variance of common developmental phenotypes [113].

Confidence: high for the algebra, which the counter-reviewer re-derived; medium for the published sizes, of which only those of [1] and [2] were re-read; low to medium for transfer [counter-review: lowered from medium]: no source treats a reference from another run or an unseen run [unverified], and the reference of [1] is one material, not a tissue.

## 3. Extrapolating short, noisy series: what beats copy-last

**Local level model.** With a random-walk level (variance s_z^2), independent observation noise s_e^2 and q = s_z^2/s_e^2, the optimal forecast is an exponentially weighted average with weight `w = (q+sqrt(q^2+4q))/(2+q+sqrt(q^2+4q))` on the last observation; copy-last is optimal only as q grows without bound [8]. First differences have variance s_z^2 + 2 s_e^2 and lag-one covariance -s_e^2 [39][31], so consecutive changes correlate at -1/(2+q) and, in this driftless model only, positive momentum has the wrong sign [unverified algebra]; with a spread of true slopes the sign turns on `t^2*d_t*d_{t+1}` against s^2 (next paragraph), and the adopted heart momentum passed its backtests [93] [counter-review]. For gaps g_t the gain is K_1 = 1, `K_t = (K_{t-1}+q*g_t)/(K_{t-1}+q*g_t+1)`, and the predicted contrast against the last stage is `-(1-K_T)*(y_T-m_{T-1})` for filtered level m, with model correlation 0.58 at q = 0 and 0.13-0.17 at q = 4 for three stages [unverified algebra; spot-checked by the screener].

**Drift against noise.** With per-gene slopes of variance t^2, sample variance s^2, increments D_t and gaps d_t, `Cov(D_t,D_{t+1}) = t^2*d_t*d_{t+1}-s^2` and `Cov(D_t,D_{t+2}) = t^2*d_t*d_{t+2}`: lag-two pairs give t^2, lag-one pairs each interior stage's s^2 [unverified algebra]. The optimal multiplier on the last increment is `k* = (t^2*d*h-s^2)/(t^2*d^2+2*s^2)` for last gap d and horizon h; per gene, `h* = clip(s-(2s+1)*v/Delta^2, -0.5, s)` for step ratio s, sample variance v and last step Delta [unverified algebra]. For a last gap of one day and a horizon of two, half the last increment beats copy-last only above |slope| = 0.93 s per day and constant velocity above 1.73 s; a line through all five heart stages lowers the break-even to 0.40-0.56 s, but its endpoint residual carries only 21% of that stage's own deviation, so on extrapolation boards the gain is a quieter slope, not a reversal [unverified algebra; re-derived by the screener]. The line's thresholds are for a forecast made from the fitted endpoint (0.53-0.62 s from the observed last value) and assume a straight trend with equal, independent noise at every stage; a trend that bends within the ladder biases a five-stage slope more than the last increment, a bend beyond it biases both, and neither cost is in these figures [counter-review; arithmetic reproduced by the counter-reviewer].

**Field tests.** On low-noise trending series smoothing buys nothing (SES 16.395 against Naive2 16.342), a full trend is worse and half-weight drift wins (Theta 14.372) [43]; on high-noise series smoothing wins [41] [counter-review: the draft's size, "by a quarter", could not be traced to a comparison and was removed]. With measurement error in the last observation the optimal coefficients shrink by reliability, `(G+S_v)^-1*G*A'`, cutting out-of-sample MSE to 0.76-0.85 of the standard model's [38]. In a held-out field test with one fixed level per unit (first-half against second-half batting averages) the current value was the worst forecast (naive 1, group mean 0.852, moment empirical Bayes 0.593), using `d_i = m+[t^2/(t^2+s_i^2)]*(X_i-m)` with variances estimated where noise and level are unrelated [37].

**Three to six points.** In a bank panel at T = 3 one-step MSE was 0.71 for pooled OLS, 0.74 for an empirical-Bayes posterior mean and 0.90 for the unit's own estimate; in the same paper's simulation with truly different units (N = 1,000, T = 3) the posterior mean's regret was 0.005 against 0.656 for pooled OLS, so which of the two wins turns on how different the units are [35] [counter-review: the two results stood in separate places]. A fixed filter beat per-series AR(1) at four points (RMSE ratio 0.78) unless the coefficient switched from positive to negative [46]; automatic selection chose at most one parameter for 127 of 144 series under 20 points [47]. In expression time courses pairwise contrasts beat time-course models below eight points [19]; a per-gene Gaussian process cannot separate smooth trend from sample noise [27], and a hierarchical one does so only through replicates [26]. Twenty-four pooled coefficients forecast 23,000 yearly series at second-place level, under bounds assuming independent series [36]; yet only 7.5% of M5 teams beat a smoothing benchmark, the rest falling short in part through "misleading validation scores" [41][42] [counter-review: reworded; the draft read as if the winners had been misled].

**Biology.** With the last four points of 48 bulk series hidden, a shared-time-axis model reached Pearson-across-time 0.367 against 0.070 by borrowing series observed at later ages (method performance only) [50]. Waddington-OT's validation supplies first-point, last-point and random-coupling nulls, with batch-to-batch distance as the noise level [53]. TRENDS models one batch per time point and pools a change the next stage does not continue, at a false-positive rate of 0.02 [48]. Closed-form OT interpolation beat 9 of 11 learned methods [49], and "it is uncommon for expression forecasting methods to out-perform simple baselines" [56].

Confidence: high for the mathematics and field results; medium-low for our ladders, whose independent sample-effect draws are one to three stage triples whatever the gene count [unverified reading]. The competitions cited date from 2011 to 2021 and none has series as short as ours; later forecasting results were not searched [counter-review].

## 4. Shape and neighbourhoods across stages

**d2_shape.** It is the p = 1 distribution-of-distances statistic on the two pairwise-distance quantile functions, a lower bound on Gromov-Wasserstein distance: `W1(D2_pred,D2_true) <= 2*GW_1` of the RMS-normalised clouds [57][58][85][91]. It is blind to mirror images and its error falls as n^(-1/2) in cells, so the scorer's 200,000 pairs do not lift the half-against-half ceiling [57][91]. D2 curves ignore 5-10% anisotropic scaling or shear and move with ellipsoid axis ratios and the separation of two pieces [59]. Chiefly spectrum, hollowness, multi-piece layout and, for a cloud of cells, the density profile can move it [unverified reading; "only" softened and density added by the counter-reviewer]. For a Gaussian cloud `Var(d^2/R^2) = 8*sum(f_k^2)`, f_k the share of RMS^2 on axis k [unverified algebra]; released stages give sum(f_k^2) of 0.34-0.51 [92] [counter-review: the figures can only be the sum, since 8*sum(f_k^2) is at least 2.67 in three dimensions]. Separately, fgw's test-board cloud is flatter than either of its brackets (thinnest axis 0.21-0.25 of RMS against 0.36 and 0.53) [93].

**Overlap and scale.** For Dice-family scores the expected-score-optimal set takes every element whose membership probability is at least half the attainable score [61][62]; on a 2019 benchmark an image-to-shape retrieval baseline beat three learned decoders in mean and median IoU and was best in 30 of 55 classes, and the authors note that a typical test shape has a very similar shape in the training set [60] [counter-review: the draft's 0.57 against 0.38-0.47 were one example's scores in the paper's first figure, not means, and were removed]. For two equally likely occupancies overlapping by ratio r, the union scores 2/(3-r) against (1+r)/2 for either alone in a fixed frame; a union also moves the PCA frame, the RMS radius and D2 [unverified algebra; re-derived by the screener]. The grid edge is 0.375 RMS, so an axis spanning +-h fills `2*ceil(h/0.375)` layers [91]; a uniformly filled stack of 30-40 sections has `h = 1.68*sqrt(f_z)` for z share f_z, with cliffs at f_z = 0.20 and 0.45, and released stages fall in three classes (0.13-0.17, 0.27-0.39, 0.51-0.68) [92] [unverified algebra]. Scale skill `|s'|/(|s'|+|s-s'|)` never puts under-prediction below the floor but does so for over-prediction beyond twice the true growth [91].

**Frames and stacks.** PCA pose normalisation is reliable only for well-elongated shapes [86][87][88]; the axis-angle SD is `rho/((rho^2-1)*sqrt(n))` radians for rho = SD1/SD2, which is 423 to 90 degrees over sqrt(n) at the released stages' rho of 1.07-1.37 [92] [unverified: formula quoted from memory; the counter-reviewer re-derived it from the large-sample variance of a principal axis, lambda1*lambda2/(n*(lambda1-lambda2)^2), which has no registry source]. Reference-free section stacking is ill-posed and "tends to straighten up structures" [63]; pipelines register slice to slice and set z from slicing depth [70][72].

**Held-out geometry.** Among the tools read, none scores a predicted geometry at a held-out stage against a copy baseline [counter-review: narrowed from "no tool predicts it"]: DeST-OT judges an alignment by growth and migration metrics [64]; STORIES passes coordinates through unchanged [65]; SOCS reports distance preservation of r 0.93 for itself, 0.92 for moscot [69] and 0.03 for expression-only transport [66]; the newest generators, STODE [67] and stMIND [68], compare only with learned models. In the sources read, a neighbour copy has been beaten only with dense ladders, replicates or training cohorts: midpoint interpolation "almost as accurate as the batch-to-batch baseline" at one-day gaps and worse at two [80][53]; nearest-stage profiles beaten on a 67-stage ladder [79]; trained frame interpolation [82].

**Matched points and neighbourhoods.** Linear interpolation shrinks parts that rotate; the remedy factors each local map A = R S and uses `R(t)*((1-t)*I+t*S)`, and a reflection has no rotation path [75], the flattening measured for mirrored partners [93]. The neighbourhood profile is the mean over a cell and its 14 nearest cells in the cloud as scored [91], a BANKSY-style mean [89] that at released densities [94] spans several sections, so arrangement below tissue-domain scale should barely move neighborhood_mmd [unverified reading].

Confidence: high for the published properties of D2, shown on surface-sampled models and not on cell clouds, and of overlap scores; medium-low for the unrun layer and spectrum arithmetic, which the counter-reviewer reproduced by hand but ran on no file; low for any claim that copying a donor shape beats generating one in our setting, which rests on [60] alone [counter-review].

## 5. Unseen knockouts: what transfers

**Staging.** In 73 embryonic-lethal lines profiled as single whole embryos at E9.5, 31.6% of homozygous embryos were delayed, and filtering against 111 somite-staged wild-type embryos shortened the response lists of 22 delayed lines by a mean 47%; delay lists overlapped by at most 30.9% between lines, and cardiovascular and nervous-system genes often stayed on schedule [95]. Age can be read per sample as `argmax_t Spearman(sample, reference(t))` on a spline-interpolated reference (R^2 0.95 against somite number in mouse embryos); the fold change expected from estimated ages correlated r = 0.8 with the observed one in one dataset [96].

**Unseen perturbations.** On 11 datasets with disjoint training and test perturbations the training mean or median was "almost always the top performer" [56]. With independently generated controls, the agreement between fold changes of different perturbations stayed positive and "far from zero" in some datasets, so a stereotyped response exists beside the shared-control artefact [56]. No deep model beat the mean or a linear model, and the one consistent winner used an embedding pretrained on another perturbation screen [97], closed to us because it would import a measured perturbation of the held-out gene [unverified reading]. A mean that also sets the targeted gene to its expected knock-down value improved a rank metric 4.7-214-fold over GEARS [99]; the targeted transcript was significantly lower in 53 of 73 knockout lines [95]. The cosine between a perturbation's shift and the mean shift averages 0.41 over ten datasets with the control as reference and -0.06 against the perturbed centroid, and two models' scores track it across datasets at r = 0.91 and 0.95 [98]; our scorer is control-referenced, so that component should be maximised on purpose and measured separately [unverified reading], with one condition: the part of a common shift that is the shared wild-type file's own deviation (section 2) transfers only to a knockout scored against that same file, and in [56] the agreement left under independent controls was far from zero in some datasets only [counter-review].

**Wild-type-only virtual knockouts.** In the papers read, most of them from 2022 and 2023, they are validated at phenotype level: CellOracle aims at the direction of cell-identity shifts [103], and an unreviewed critique reports that shuffling its base network changed its benchmark insignificantly [104]; GenKI's real-knockout checks are overlaps of 8 and 17 with the top 50 differential genes, without direction [105]; scTenifoldKnk reports recapitulating "main findings" [106]. The best held-out evidence found is at fate level, Spearman 0.35 over 14 single-factor knockouts, with no naive baseline [107].

**Generic priors.** A prior from 635 human studies predicts a held-out study's differential list at AUROC 0.83, but not its signs [101]; same-gene effects correlate 0.40-0.75 across cell types once sampling covariance is modelled [100].

**Off limits.** Resources built from perturbing the held-out genes or a phenocopy stay closed: gene sets and footprints named for them or their pathway, transcription-factor perturbation libraries, genome-wide Perturb-seq atlases with models trained on them, regulons of the held-out factors, and one foundation model's cardiac in-silico-deletion showcase [unverified: the researcher's prior knowledge; none was opened].

Confidence: high for the benchmark results, of which [56] and [98] were re-read and [95] could not be; low to medium for transfer from cell-line screens with many training perturbations and from whole-embryo bulk to one imaging sample per condition and two released knockout lines [counter-review: lowered from medium].

## 6. Implementable recipes, ranked

Common rules. Recipes use released files only (wild-type ladders, the released Gata4 and Mab21l2 knockouts, unlocked validation stages): no outside measurement from the barred windows, no outside gene-level knockout result. Each needs a ledger row before any run and the captain's ruling before an entry changes; these rules, like the Legal lines below, are as the researchers stated them, and Effect lines are expectations [unverified]. Before taking residuals (R2, R3, R8), remove the part monotone in reference level, which de_direction partials out [34]. Order: diagnostics that can close other lines, wild-type-only T3 work, ladders, shape, low-identifiability items [unverified: the screener's ordering].

**R1. Noise budget and replicate ceiling (all boards; diagnostic).** *In:* counted backtests; the two Gata4 replicate runs. *Do:* split each backtest contrast's energy into truth noise, reference noise reachable by reversion, and persistent change; score candidates by `TSE* = (sum((y_T-d)^2)-sum(S_T))` over the same for copy-last [37]; add a reference-regressed-to-its-ladder row and a specific-skill row net of the leave-one-out mean shift [98]; score replicate 1 as a prediction of replicate 2 on every metric [53]. *Effect:* no points; shows which metric can rank candidates and how much credit is free. *Kill:* a metric whose replicate-against-replicate skill is not above copy-last's cannot rank candidates; a reference-noise share of a few percent closes R2 and R3 unrun. *For:* a mean predictor scored correlation 0.649 in a simulation of pure noise with fold changes taken against controls [56]. *Against:* with two knockout lines the leave-one-out mean is one file [98]. *Legal:* target-free.

**R2. Reference-residual factor for an interior reference (T3, then T2).** *In:* wild-type heart files from E8.25 to E10.5; both released knockouts. *Do:* per gene, in the scorer's pseudobulk, `r_875 = pb(E8.75)-0.75*pb(E8.5)-0.25*pb(E9.5)` and `r_95 = pb(E9.5)-0.571*pb(E8.75)-0.429*pb(E10.5)`; split each into a scalar, a label-shared gene part and a remainder; predict `dp = -L*r`, L fitted leave-one-stage-out or on untouched lineages and capped at `1/(1+sum(w^2))`, 0.62 and 0.66 [unverified algebra]; these caps, and the ceilings below, hold only if expression is linear in stage between the two neighbours, because real stage-specific expression also enters r, is shared by a knockout of that stage and does not revert [counter-review]. *Effect:* de_direction and de_score on T3; correlation with the sample-effect part of a delta at most 0.55-0.58 alone, about 0.60 with the existing factor (weights 0.44 on -r, 0.28 on the factor) [unverified algebra; re-derived by the screener]. *Kill:* predict each released knockout's delta from the ladder alone: the partial rank correlation of -r with the true delta must be positive on both Gata4 replicates and Mab21l2, de_direction must rise, and the wrong stage's residual must do worse; if both Gata4 replicates are scored against one wild-type file they are one test of this sign, not two [counter-review]. *For:* [1][2][3], which support the shared-reference algebra and a same-material reference, not this estimator [counter-review]. *Against:* no source uses an interior-stage residual as a noise estimate [counter-review]; correction can cost more variance than the bias it removes [7]; the ledger closed a neighbouring idea at the heart endpoint, where leverage was 0.80-0.91 [93]. *Legal:* T3's rules are frozen and the scalar part touches the closed depth lever: diagnostic until the captain rules.

**R3. Ladder moments, then a pooled coefficient on the last step (T2 heart; T1 only where the protocol is unchanged).** *In:* per-type log pseudobulks at released stages with jackknife or bootstrap sampling variances. *Do:* centre increments on expression-decile medians; estimate t^2 and s^2 from section 3's moments and apply k*, allowed negative, or h* per gene with Delta^2 shrunk to its class mean; at the reference, shrink the last innovation by a gain `K = P/(P+R_g)` estimated by moments within deciles [37], guarding the top of the list with a heavy-tailed prior [55]. *Effect:* de_direction, since the rule changes signs. *Kill:* leave-one-triple-out, where the sign flip must lose; kill if the slope changes sign between triples or the counted backtests do not beat copy-last in 9 of 10 seeds. *For:* [8][35][38][39]. *Against:* three RNA stages cannot separate t^2 from s^2, and T1's last step crosses a lasting dissection change [unverified reading]; a gene that truly switches on at the last stage is reversed [48]; in simulation at T = 3 a predictor built on the last difference had by far the largest regret of the four quoted in Annex A [35] [counter-review]; the adopted heart momentum passed its backtests [93]. *Legal:* released stages only.

**R4. Level-dependent sample-variance calibration for ranking (T3 first).** *In:* the two Gata4 replicate runs; knockout-minus-wild-type contrasts in untouched lineages. *Do:* take D = replicate 1 - replicate 2 for gene-by-type units in windows of 150-200 by level [21]; replicate-difference variance per file = robust Var(D)/2, which already holds run variance if the two replicates are separate runs and must then not be added again [counter-review: the draft called it within-run variance]; between-run variance from untouched-lineage contrasts less sampling variance; shrink each predicted delta by signal/(signal + sampling + run variance) and rank genes by the probability of exceeding the scorer's 0.25 threshold with the right sign [unverified algebra]. *Effect:* de_score lists. *Kill:* the shrunken ranking must raise de_score on both cross-knockout folds, and replicate 1 against replicate 2 must call almost nothing. *For:* unreplicated analyses borrow variance from a chosen coefficient of variation or from control transcripts [9]; [23][55]. *Against:* no single-subject method passed 90% precision against replicate-based references [20]. *Legal:* T3 is frozen: ruling.

**R5. Difference-in-differences lineage factor (T3).** *In:* released knockout and wild-type files; driver-lineage labels L, untouchable labels N. *Do:* `D_k(g) = [KO-WT in L]-[KO-WT in N]` with bootstrap standard errors; rho disattenuated as in section 2 [100]; `G = c*mean_k(D_k)`, `c = K*rho/(1+(K-1)*rho)`, 0.26 at rho 0.15 and 0.57 at 0.4 for two knockouts [unverified algebra]; add G to lineage labels. *Effect:* de_direction and de_score in lineage labels; run effects shared by L and N cancel. *Kill:* G from Mab21l2 must predict D of each Gata4 replicate, and the reverse, with rho's interval above zero. *For:* within-sample designs [111]; Cre alone has effects [112][117]. *Against:* heterozygotes showed "no, or only a very small" response [95]; the perturbation-specific part is predicted at r = 0.20-0.26 even inside one screen [33]; the Gata4 line also carries one floxed Gata6 allele [34]; the organisers' gene_ko baseline sits below the floor on the Gata4 board [33]. *Legal:* an optional step carrying the knocked-out gene's own readout ratio speaks about the held-out gene's transcript, though built from released files: captain's ruling first.

**R6. Two upload geometries chosen by expected best-of-two shape skill (T2).** *In:* released stages; the menu's clouds (fgw, fgw_shape0, fgw_shape1); the emulator's shape functions. *Do:* scenarios = donor outline (either bracket) x thin-axis share on a grid over released values plus a margin; pseudo-truth = the donor's cloud, subsampled as the emulator does and rescaled along its own principal axes to the scenario's spectrum, shares interpolated between the brackets' principal SDs [90]; choose the pair maximising `sum(w*max(skill_1,skill_2))`; guides: the median distance-quantile target [58] and the inclusion rule [61]; audit occupied layers and frame angles. *Effect:* d2_shape and occupancy_dice; 0.1 skill on one shape metric is 0.83 of a board point before the structure gate [91]. *Kill:* replayed on each counted backtest from its brackets alone, the chosen pair must at least equal (fgw, fgw_shape0); the self-rescaling Dice curve must step at the predicted factors. *For:* [57][59]; [60] only weakly, being from 2019 and tied to near-copies between training and test shapes [counter-review]. *Against:* row E11 lost on E7.25 and rows E16/E17 found no bracket geometry beating fgw [93]. *Legal:* target-free; build scenarios from the emulator's own subsampling only.

**R7. Per-label transcriptomic age (T3).** *In:* released samples and labels. *Do:* estimate age per label by section 5's argmax on an interpolated reference, wild-type samples staged leave-one-out, intervals from 30 bootstraps on a third of genes [96], or per cell as the mean stage of its nearest wild-type neighbours [102]; age component = reference(age_KO) - reference(age_ref); for the held-out knockout, a shrunk mean delay per label class. *Effect:* de_direction; small; its value is the decomposition. *Kill:* leave-one-out ages must keep nominal order within half the stage spacing; the lineage-minus-non-lineage delay must agree in sign between the Gata4 replicates and Mab21l2. *For:* [95][96]. *Against:* cardiovascular genes often stayed on schedule [95]; a mis-staged litter mimics delay [102]; our own check found the reference on its nominal stage [32]; the held-out line's reported phenotype includes no general growth retardation [33]. *Legal:* reference from released stages only; phenotype-level knowledge is allowed.

## 7. Key controversies

**Reverting the reference's deviation pays.** For: the closed forms [2][3][7]; reliability-shrunk forecasts won out of sample [38]; the naive rule was worst in a field test [37]. Against: the corrected slope has twice the sampling variance at N = 100 and breaks down below N = 50 [7]; on low-noise series smoothing is no better than naive [43], and "naive forecasts are hard to beat" for 518 annual tourism series [44]; reagent ageing grows with run duration and imaged area, so for the genes it governs a trend has the right sign and a reversal the wrong one [11].

**One per-gene run effect serves all cell types.** For: such an effect was recovered at R^2 0.90 [22], though between a sequencing reference and a spatial assay, not between runs [counter-review: recollection, see section 2]. Against: run effects were cell-type specific in three studies [4][5][6], and run-to-run correlations of 0.94-1.00 may leave a remainder too small to move rank metrics [10][12][14][15].

**Simple baselines always win for unseen perturbations.** For: [56][97][98]; nearest-neighbour baselines are competitive for differential-expression classification [109]. Against: about 30% of evaluations across nine CRISPR datasets resist simple baselines, more so for unusual responses [108]; linear approaches miss perturbation-specific effects [116]; this side rests on one abstract and one search summary, both of authority 5 [counter-review].

**Delay explains a mutant's response.** For: 47% of response lists [95]; r = 0.8 [96]. Against: only 31.6% of homozygous embryos were delayed and cardiovascular genes often stayed on schedule [95]; a mis-staged litter can be the simpler reading [102]; mutant-mRNA decay can raise sequence-similar genes [114], and linked genes can differ by allele-specific expression [115].

Confidence: medium; three of the four turn on quantities only our own data can measure.

Researchers' derivations and proposals (none run):

- One-triple moments [39] [unverified algebra]: with gaps g_1, g_2 and no drift spread, `s_e^2 = -Cov(d_1,d_2)` and `q = (Var(d_j)/s_e^2 - 2)/g_j`; with drift spread t_b^2 the covariance is `g_1*g_2*t_b^2 - s_e^2`, so one triple gives only the sign of the balance, and at equal gaps the pooled slope of the next difference on the last is `(t_b^2 - s_e^2)/(t_b^2 + s_z^2 + 2*s_e^2)`. Each triple is one draw of the sample effects, whatever the gene count.

Estimators as the sources give them:

- RUV-III [16]: with M mapping assays to replicate sets, `R = I - M(M'M)^-1 M'`; U_k = top k eigenvectors of `R Y Y' R`; `alpha = U_k' Y`; `W = Yc alpha_c' (alpha_c alpha_c')^-1` on the centred control genes; `Y_adj = Y - W alpha`.
- CellANOVA [6]: `X_i = C_i M + B_i V' + T_i W' + Z_i`; regress each sample on cell-state encodings, centre the coefficient matrices within the control pool, take their SVD for V, correct by `X_i - (X_i - C_i M) V V'`; signal aligned with the batch space is lost by design.
- MOUTHWASH [17]: `beta_hat ~ N(beta + alpha' z, xi S)`, beta unimodal at zero, z by weighted least squares on loadings learned from other samples.
- Single-subject calls [21]: local dispersion in windows of 150-200 genes by level, variance-stabilise, z per gene, empirical null, local false discovery rate; precision 0.93 and recall 0.65 in the authors' simulation, against 0.31 and 0.96 for replicate-free edgeR.
- Tweedie posterior mean [35]: `E[l_i | data] = l_hat_i + (s^2/T) d ln p(l_hat_i, y_i0)/d l_hat_i`; simulated regrets at T = 3 were 0.005 for it, 0.358 for the plug-in, 0.656 for pooled OLS and 2.963 for a first-difference predictor.
- Measurement error [38]: scalar AR(1) coefficient `a*s_y^2/(s_y^2 + s_v^2)`; the latest observation is worth using only if `s_v^2 < s_e^2/(1 - a^2)`.
- TRENDS [48]: `F_i^-1 = G_l^-1 + E_i` per batch, G monotone over time at every quantile, fitted by Wasserstein least squares with pool-adjacent-violators; it needs about 50 cells per batch and cannot represent pulsed or cyclic patterns.
- Partial alignment [71]: transport a mass fraction s only (`pi 1 <= g`, `pi' 1 <= g'`, `1' pi 1 = s`), with s under- rather than over-estimated.
- Systema [98]: deltas against the mean of perturbation centroids; centroid accuracy = share of other perturbations lying farther from the prediction than the true one.
- Linear baseline [97]: `Y_hat = G W P' + b`, `W = (G'G + lambda I)^-1 G' (Y - b) P (P'P + lambda I)^-1`, lambda = 0.1, K = 10.
- Generic differential-expression prior [101]: score of a gene = sum over hit lists containing it of 1/(Npos x Nneg).
- Staging: three fits (mutants against siblings; plus stage-matched baseline embryos; plus somite number as a factor) sort genes into response, delay, no-delay and discard classes [95]; the reference is interpolated by spline models on component scores, and estimates within 5% of its edges are flagged [96].

- RUV4 inflated false discoveries, and two published negative-control gene sets gave different hit lists [4]. RUV-III tolerated 20-80% shuffled biological labels [16].
